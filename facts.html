Types of animation

There are many different types of animation, here's a few you can check out right now!

  1. 3D animation

    This type of animation creates characters and objects with height, width, and depth, making them appear three-dimensional. Animators use computer software to build models, add textures, and move characters. It is often used in animated movies, video games, and visual effects.

  2. 2D animation

    This type of animation is flat in appearance, It creates movement using images that have height and width, giving them a flat appearance. It brings characters and stories to life through drawings and digital illustrations, and is often used in cartoons, anime, and animated shows.

  3. Stop-Motion Animation

    This type of animation uses real objects, puppets, or models that are moved slightly and photographed one frame at a time. When the images are played in sequence, the objects appear to move. Claymation is a popular example of stop-motion animation.

  4. Traditional Animation

    This type of animation involves drawing individual images by hand, with small changes between each frame to create movement. These drawings are shown in sequence to bring characters and scenes to life. Traditional animation has been used in many classic animated films and cartoons.

  5. Motion Graphics

    This type of animation brings text, shapes, icons, and other graphic elements to life through movement. It is often used in advertisements, title sequences, presentations, and educational videos to make information more engaging.

Keyframes and In-Betweening

A keyframe is an important frame that shows a particular position or moment in an animation. Animators use keyframes to plan the main movements of a character or object.

In-betweening is the process of creating the frames between keyframes. These extra frames help connect the main poses and make the movement look smoother. Depending on the animation software and technique, in-between frames can be created manually or generated digitally.

Frames Per Second (FPS)

FPS stands for frames per second. It describes how many individual frames are displayed every second. A traditional animated motion picture commonly uses a playback rate of 24 frames per second. However, animators do not always need to draw a new image for every frame. Some drawings can be held across multiple frames, depending on the style and movement.

Rigging in 3D Animation

Rigging is the process of creating a digital skeleton and control system for a 3D character. Animators use these controls to move different parts of the character, such as the arms, legs, and head. Without a suitable rig, moving a detailed 3D character can be much more difficult.

2D and Digital Animation

2D animation creates movement using images in two dimensions: height and width. It often has a flat appearance compared with 3D animation, which represents objects with height, width, and depth.

Digital animation can be used in many different ways, including video games, interactive advertisements, and clickable tutorials. It allows creators to communicate ideas, tell stories, and make experiences more engaging.

Stop-Motion Animation

Stop-motion animation creates movement by physically moving objects a little at a time and photographing each position. When the photographs are played in sequence, the objects appear to move by themselves.

Claymation is one type of stop-motion animation that uses characters made from clay or similar materials. Puppet animation is another type, using puppets that are carefully repositioned and photographed one frame at a time.

Arcs and Natural Movement

Many natural movements follow curved paths called arcs rather than perfectly straight lines. For example, when a character swings their arm or throws a ball, the movement often follows a curve. Using arcs helps make animation feel more natural and fluid.

Rotoscoping

Rotoscoping is an animation technique in which animators trace over live-action footage, frame by frame, to help create movement. It can be useful for studying realistic movement and reproducing it in animation. Tracing can also be used as a practice exercise to develop drawing and observation skills.
